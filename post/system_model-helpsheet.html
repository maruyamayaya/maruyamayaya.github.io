2. Stable Distributions, Power Laws, and Pareto

Stability and characteristic functions: for a sum of i.i.d. variables, stability is written Sn=danX+bnS_n\overset{d}{=}a_nX+b_nSn​=dan​X+bn​. The characteristic function (CF), φX(q)=E[eiqX]\varphi_X(q)=E[e^{iqX}]φX​(q)=E[eiqX], always exists.

For the symmetric stable family centered at 000, take c>0c>0c>0 and 0<α≤20<\alpha\le20<α≤2:

φX(q)=e−c∣q∣α,Sn=dn1/αX,X‾=dn1/α−1X.\begin{aligned} \varphi_X(q)&=e^{-c|q|^\alpha},\\ S_n&\overset{d}{=}n^{1/\alpha}X,\\ \overline X&\overset{d}{=}n^{1/\alpha-1}X. \end{aligned} φX​(q)Sn​X​=e−c∣q∣α,=dn1/αX,=dn1/α−1X.​

At α=2\alpha=2α=2, the distribution is Gaussian with variance 2c2c2c. At α=1\alpha=1α=1, it is Cauchy:

pC(x)=cπ(x2+c2),φC(q)=e−c∣q∣,X‾=dX.\begin{aligned} p_C(x)&=\frac{c}{\pi(x^2+c^2)},\\ \varphi_C(q)&=e^{-c|q|},\\ \overline X&\overset{d}{=}X. \end{aligned} pC​(x)φC​(q)X​=π(x2+c2)c​,=e−c∣q∣,=dX.​

The Cauchy mean is undefined. A symmetric principal value of 000 does not establish the existence of an expectation. The sample mean does not become concentrated as sample size increases.

The PDF and CCDF have log-log slopes −γ-\gamma−γ and −α-\alpha−α, respectively. Moments diverge at equality. A power-law tail with tail index α=2\alpha=2α=2 still has a divergent second moment. Within the stable family, the power-law tail and this moment criterion apply only for 0<α<20<\alpha<20<α<2. Stability index α=2\alpha=2α=2 is the Gaussian exception, with finite moments of every order.

Complex-systems keywords: many-body, autonomous, nonlinear interaction, emergence. Observing a power law alone does not establish its generating mechanism.

4. BA and ER Networks

Let nnn be the number of nodes, MMM the total number of edges, and mmm the number of edges introduced by each new node in the BA model.

BA: growth plus linear preferential attachment.

n=n0+t,M=M0+mt,Πi=ki∑jkj,∑jkj≃2mt.\begin{aligned} n&=n_0+t,\\ M&=M_0+mt,\\ \Pi_i&=\frac{k_i}{\sum_jk_j},\\ \sum_jk_j&\simeq2mt. \end{aligned} nMΠi​j∑​kj​​=n0​+t,=M0​+mt,=∑j​kj​ki​​,≃2mt.​

Birth times are approximately uniform. For k≥mk\ge mk≥m, inversion gives ti=t(m/k)2t_i=t(m/k)^2ti​=t(m/k)2, hence
